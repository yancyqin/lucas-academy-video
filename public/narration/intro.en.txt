What happens when the same monkeys live under different rules?

Monkey Republic is not just a game about bananas. It is a small society where students can test how trust, memory, rewards, and betrayal shape behavior.

Every monkey has only two choices: share, or grab. But the numbers behind those choices change the world.

In one world, sharing creates extra bananas. Trust has room to grow. In another world, sharing still helps a little, but betrayal hurts badly.

The first few rounds are slow. Students can see who shared, who grabbed, how many bananas changed hands, and why one choice affects the next one.

Then the computer runs the society faster and faster. After a hundred rounds, patterns appear. Some monkeys become rich. Some lose trust. Some learn what the world rewards.

The takeaway is not a simple sentence. It is a question. Now that we can see how rules train strategy, how do we understand ourselves?

In the age of AI, learning is not just about skills. It is about thinking with curiosity, creating through discovery, searching for significance, and contributing to real-world problems that matter.

Monkey Republic. A Lucas Academy social experiment.
