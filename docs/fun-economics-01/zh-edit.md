一句话主线：**一样东西的价钱，除了看得见的，还有看不见的。** 第一种看不见，在选择的时候：是放下的那件事（冰棍外面那两块钱）。

直接拿答案，放下的就是这些想法。

## OC00 · 开场：一根冰棍，要花多少钱？

段落语气: 

- oc00-01 | 小卖部的冰棍，一根卖五块钱。Mary，买一根冰棍，要花多少钱？
  > An ice pop at the corner shop costs 5 yuan. Mary, how much does it cost to buy one ice pop?
- oc00-02 | Mary：姐姐，这还用问吗？当然是五块钱呀！
  > Do you even need to ask? 5 yuan, of course!
- oc00-03 | 没错。可是去小卖部，一来一回要走三十分钟。
  > Right. But the walk to the corner store and back takes 30 minutes.
- oc00-04 | 妈妈说，这三十分钟要是在家做家务，就奖励你两块钱零花钱。
  > And Mom says, if you spend those 30 minutes doing chores at home, she'll give you 2 yuan of pocket money.
- oc00-05 | 现在再算一算：买一根冰棍，到底要花多少钱？
  > Now let's count again: how much does buying that ice pop really cost?
- oc00-06 | Mary：嗯……五块钱，再加上……没拿到的那两块钱？
  > Umm... 5 yuan, plus... the 2 yuan I didn't get?
- oc00-07 | 对，一共七块钱。买冰棍是这样，那学一样东西，又要花多少钱呢？
  > Yes, 7 yuan in all. That's an ice pop. So how much does it cost to learn something?

## OC01 · 机会成本：没拿到的那两块钱

段落语气: 

- oc01-01 | 那两块钱，你没有掏出来，可为了去买冰棍，你把它放下了。经济学家管这个叫<strong>机会成本</strong>。
  > You never paid those 2 yuan, but to go buy the ice pop, you gave them up. Economists call this opportunity cost.
- oc01-02 | 五块钱看得见，两块钱看不见。冰棍真正的价钱，是两样加起来。
  > The 5 yuan you can see. The 2 yuan you can't. The real price of the ice pop is the two together.
- oc01-03 | 那三十分钟，你也可以看书、睡午觉、踢球。可三十分钟只够做一件事，所以机会成本只算你最想要的那一个，不是全部加起来。
  > In those 30 minutes, you could also read a book, take a nap, or play soccer. But 30 minutes is only enough for one of them, so the opportunity cost is only the one you wanted most, not all of them added up.
- oc01-04 | 就算冰棍是免费的，那两块钱也还是没有了。所以经济学家常说：天下没有免费的午餐。
  > Even if the ice pop were free, those 2 yuan would still be gone. That's why economists say: there's no such thing as a free lunch.
- oc01-05 | 同一根冰棍，对不同的人，价钱也不一样：住在小卖部隔壁的孩子，走两分钟就到；住得远的孩子，这根冰棍就更贵。
  > And the same ice pop has a different price for different people: a kid who lives next door gets there in 2 minutes; for a kid who lives far away, the ice pop costs more.

## OC02 · 学一样东西，也一样

段落语气: 

- oc02-01 | 学一样东西也一样。Mary 每个星期六下午学一小时钢琴。学费看得见；看不见的，是这一小时本来可以做的事。
  > Learning something is the same. Every Saturday afternoon, Mary has a one-hour piano lesson. The fee you can see; what you can't see is what that hour could have been.
- oc02-02 | 她弹会的曲子，大家都听得见；没有做的事，谁也看不见。
  > Everyone can hear the pieces she learns to play; the things she didn't do, nobody sees.
- oc02-03 | 1850 年，法国经济学家巴斯夏写过一篇文章，叫《看得见的与看不见的》。他说，不好的经济学家只看见看得见的；好的经济学家，还会去想那些看不见的。
  > In 1850, the French economist Frédéric Bastiat wrote an essay called “That Which Is Seen, and That Which Is Not Seen”. He said a bad economist sees only what can be seen; a good economist also thinks about what can't.
- oc02-04 | 一天只有 24 个小时，就像 24 枚时间币，每一枚只能花一次。睡觉、上学、吃饭花掉一大半，剩下能自己选的，往往只有三四枚。
  > A day has only 24 hours, like 24 time coins, and each coin can be spent only once. Sleep, school and meals use up most of them; the ones left for you to choose are often just three or four.
- oc02-05 | 时间不够用，选择才有代价。经济学管这个叫<strong>稀缺</strong>。
  > Because there isn't enough time, every choice has a cost. Economists call this scarcity.
- oc02-06 | 所以爸爸妈妈帮孩子选课的时候，也可以把看不见的那一半放上天平：多报一个班，看得见的是学费和本领；看不见的，是那个下午原本会做的事，也许是自由地玩，也许是一家人一起吃顿饭。
  > So when parents choose classes for their children, they can put the unseen half on the scale too. One more class brings a fee and a new skill you can see; what you can't see is what that afternoon would have been: maybe free play, maybe a family dinner.
- oc02-07 | 这不是说不该学，而是选的时候，两边都看见。
  > This doesn't mean you shouldn't learn. It means that when you choose, you see both sides.

## OC04 · 7 乘 8，有几条路？

段落语气: 

- oc04-01 | Mary 还没上二年级，就把乘法口诀背得滚瓜烂熟。老师问：7 乘 8 等于多少？
  > Before second grade, Mary already knew her times tables by heart. Her teacher asked: what is 7 times 8?
- oc04-02 | Mary：七八五十六！
  > Seven eights are fifty-six!
- oc04-03 | 老师又问：你是怎么算出来的？
  > Then her teacher asked: how did you work it out?
- oc04-04 | Mary：口诀里就是这么说的呀！
  > That's what the times table says!
- oc04-05 | 答案是对的。可如果还没背过口诀，7 乘 8，你会怎么算？其实有好几条路。我们都画在同一张点子图上：8 行，每行 7 个点。
  > The answer is right. But if you hadn't learned the times table yet, how would you work out 7 times 8? There are actually several roads. Let's draw them all on the same dot picture: 8 rows, with 7 dots in each row.
- oc04-06 | 第一条路：连加。一行一行加起来：7、14、21、28、35、42、49、56。它练的是：乘法到底是什么。
  > Road one: add equal groups. Add it up row by row: 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49, 56. What it trains: what multiplying really means.
- oc04-07 | 第二条路：拆开再合。竖着切一刀，左边 8 乘 5 是 40，右边 8 乘 2 是 16，合起来是 56。它练的是：把难的拆成好算的。以后的两位数乘法，就是这么算的。
  > Road two: split and join. Cut it from top to bottom: on the left, 8 times 5 is 40; on the right, 8 times 2 is 16; together, 56. What it trains: breaking a hard one into easy ones. Later, that's exactly how you'll multiply two-digit numbers.
- oc04-08 | 第三条路，用手指：从 10 往回看，也能算出 56；为什么可以这样算，我们单独用一个视频来讲。
  > Path three: use your fingers, counting back from 10, and you get 56 too; why that works gets a video of its own.
- oc04-15 | 第四条路，只用翻倍和减半。三千多年前的古埃及人，算乘法就是靠翻倍。
  > Road four: only doubling and halving. Over three thousand years ago, the ancient Egyptians multiplied by doubling.
- oc04-16 | 左边写 7，每次减半，去掉余数：7、3、1。右边写 8，每次翻倍：8、16、32。左边是单数的行，右边就留下。这三行都是单数：8 加 16 加 32，等于 56。
  > Write 7 on the left and halve it each time, dropping any remainder: 7, 3, 1. Write 8 on the right and double it each time: 8, 16, 32. Wherever the left side is odd, keep the right side. All three rows are odd: 8 plus 16 plus 32 equals 56.
- oc04-17 | 为什么？把点子图横过来：7 行，每行 8 个。
  > Why? Turn the dot picture on its side: 7 rows of 8.
- oc04-18 | 7 是单数，先拿走 1 行，是 8。剩下 6 行，两行并成一份，变成 3 份，每份 16。
  > 7 is odd, so first take away 1 row: that's 8. The 6 rows left pair up into 3 groups of 16.
- oc04-19 | 3 是单数，再拿走 1 份，是 16。剩下 2 份，并成 1 份，是 32，也拿走。
  > 3 is odd, so take away 1 group: that's 16. The 2 groups left join into 1 group of 32, and we take that too.
- oc04-20 | 左边减半，是份数；右边翻倍，是每份有多大；遇到单数，就拿走一份。8 加 16 加 32，一个点都不少。
  > Halving on the left counts the groups; doubling on the right is how big each group is; whenever the count is odd, take one group away. 8 plus 16 plus 32: not a single dot is missing.
- oc04-21 | 它练的是：只用翻倍，也能拼出任何一个数。计算机里的二进制，用的就是这个道理。
  > What it trains: doubling alone can build any number. The binary numbers inside a computer work the same way.
- oc04-22 | 四条路，四种想法：乘法的意思，拆成好算的，从 10 往回看，用翻倍拼出任何数。
  > Four roads, four ways of thinking: what multiplying means, breaking it into easy parts, counting back from 10, and building any number by doubling.
- oc04-23 | 这些路，都要在还不知道答案的时候，才会去走。Mary 先背下了七八五十六，就不会再去走了。
  > You only walk these roads while you don't know the answer yet. Mary memorized “seven eights are fifty-six” first, so she won't walk them anymore.
- oc04-24 | 她放下的，就是这四种想法。这也是<strong>机会成本</strong>，只是它特别难看见，因为答案是对的。
  > What she gave up is these four ways of thinking. That's opportunity cost too, only it's extra hard to see, because the answer is right.

## OC05 · 先走路，再背口诀

段落语气: 

- oc05-01 | 这不是说不要背口诀。口诀背得熟，以后算大题才快。
  > This doesn't mean you shouldn't learn the times table. Knowing it well makes bigger problems faster later on.
- oc05-02 | 只是顺序很重要：先自己动脑筋，走一走这些路，再把口诀背熟。那时候，「七八五十六」就不只是一句顺口溜，你知道它是从哪里来的。
  > It's just that the order matters: first think for yourself and walk these roads, then learn the table by heart. Then “seven eights are fifty-six” won't just be a chant; you'll know where it comes from.
- oc05-03 | 今天我们找到了两种看不见的价钱：选择的时候，是放下的那件事，就像那两块钱；学习的时候，是直接拿答案，放下的那些想法。
  > Today we found two prices you can't see: when you choose, it's the thing you put down, like those 2 yuan; when you learn, it's the ideas you give up by going straight to the answer.
- oc05-04 | 看见看不见的，这是经济学家的眼睛，也是会学习的人的眼睛。
  > Seeing what can't be seen: that's an economist's eye, and a good learner's eye too.
- oc05-05 | 对了，如果你想：我已经学了三年，现在换，前面不就白学了？已经花掉的时间拿不回来，它该不该决定下一步怎么走呢？这叫<strong>沉没成本</strong>，我们下一次再聊。
  > Oh, and if you're thinking: I've already studied for three years, so if I switch now, wasn't it all for nothing? The time you've spent won't come back, so should it decide your next step? That's called a sunk cost, and we'll talk about it next time.

## OC06 · 彩蛋：马大和马利亚

段落语气: 

- oc06-01 | 说起来，Mary 这个名字，两千年前就有一位。她和姐姐，也遇到过这样的选择。
  > By the way, there was a Mary two thousand years ago, too. She and her sister faced a choice just like this.
- oc06-02 | 耶稣来到她们家。姐姐马大忙着准备饭菜；妹妹马利亚，英文名字就是 Mary，坐在耶稣脚前听道。马大忙不过来，就请耶稣叫妹妹来帮忙。
  > Jesus came to their home. Her sister Martha was busy getting the meal ready, while Mary, the same name as ours, sat at Jesus' feet and listened to him. Martha couldn't keep up, so she asked Jesus to tell her sister to help.
- oc06-03 | 耶稣说：「马大！马大！你为许多的事思虑烦扰，但是不可少的只有一件；马利亚已经选择那上好的福分，是不能夺去的。」
  > Jesus said: “Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is needed. Mary has chosen the good part, which will not be taken away from her.”
- oc06-04 | 招待客人是好事，坐下来听也是好事。最难的选择，往往在两件好事之间。被放下的那件也很好的时候，机会成本最高。
  > Welcoming guests is good; sitting down to listen is good too. The hardest choices are often between two good things. When the thing you put down is good too, the opportunity cost is highest.

## OC07 · 结尾的问题

段落语气: 

- oc07-01 | 那么，下一次花掉三十分钟的时候，你会看见那个被放下的选项吗？当你背下几个单词、记住一个公式，或者让 AI 直接写出一段程序的时候，你知道自己放下了什么样的机会吗？在 Lucas Academy，我们在乎你学会了什么，更在乎你在学的过程中，学会怎样思考。
  > So the next time you spend 30 minutes, will you see the choice you put down? When you memorize a few words, learn a formula by heart, or let AI write a program for you, do you know what chance you're giving up? At Lucas Academy, we care about what you learn, and we care even more that, while you learn, you learn how to think.
