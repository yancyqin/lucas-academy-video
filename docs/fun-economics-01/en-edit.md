写法和中文稿一样：`<strong>…</strong>` 是强调，字幕里自动去掉；以「Mary:」开头的是 Mary 的台词（前缀不读、字幕不出）。
每句下面 `>` 开头的是这句的中文翻译：英文片的中文字幕用它，不读。钱和名字跟英文片一致（美元）。编号、句数和中文一致（57 句），画面和网页步骤跟着编号走。

v0.8：手指那条路和中文一样只剩 oc04-08 一句，原理另做视频（Can You Work Out 7 × 8?）。

英文版的改动（方案第 8 节）：
- 钱换成美元：冰棍 $2，三十分钟家务 $1，一共 $3（中文是 ¥5 + ¥2 = ¥7，比例不同，道理一样）。卡片、封面跟着改。
- 「七八五十六」用英文背乘法表的说法 Seven eights are fifty-six；口诀 = the times table。四条路（path）：add equal groups、split and join、use your fingers、double and halve。
- 巴斯夏的文章用英文通行的标题 “That Which Is Seen, and That Which Is Not Seen”。
- 路加福音 10:41–42 用 WEB（World English Bible，公有领域），已对过原文。英文里 Mary 本来就是同一个名字，所以 oc06-02 不再解释名字。
- 「拆开来乘」= split it up，呼应第二条路 split and join。
- 「路」英文用 path：Louise 读的 road 四处被听成 rows / row / route（v0.7 核对）。「十个十」说 ten groups of ten（ten tens 被听成 tenths）。
- can't 在三句里被听成 can，意思反了：这三句配音读 cannot，字幕不变。

读法（配音时写进 SPOKEN，字幕不变）：数字、$、×、−、+ 都拼成英文词；1850 读 eighteen fifty；Bastiat 读法按法语，SPOKEN 写 Bastia。

## OC00 · Opening: How much does an ice pop cost?

- oc00-01 | An ice pop at the corner store costs $2. Mary, how much does it cost to buy one ice pop?
  > 小卖部的冰棍，一根卖 2 美元。Mary，买一根冰棍，要花多少钱？
- oc00-02 | Mary: That's easy! $2, of course!
  > 这还不简单！当然是 2 美元呀！
- oc00-03 | That's right. But the walk to the corner store and back takes 30 minutes.
  > 没错。可是去小卖部，一来一回要走三十分钟。
- oc00-04 | And Mom says, if you spend those 30 minutes doing chores at home, she'll give you $1 of pocket money.
  > 妈妈说，这 30 分钟要是在家做家务，就奖励你 1 美元零花钱。
- oc00-05 | Now let's count again: how much does buying that ice pop really cost?
  > 现在再算一算：买一根冰棍，到底要花多少钱？
- oc00-06 | Mary: Umm... $2, plus... the dollar I didn't get?
  > 嗯……2 美元，再加上……没拿到的那 1 美元？
- oc00-07 | Yes, $3 in all. That's an ice pop. So how much does it cost to learn something?
  > 对，一共 3 美元。买冰棍是这样，那学一样东西，又要花多少钱呢？

## OC01 · Opportunity cost: the dollar you didn't get

- oc01-01 | You never paid that dollar, but to go buy the ice pop, you gave it up. Economists call this <strong>opportunity cost</strong>.
  > 那 1 美元，你没有掏出来，可为了去买冰棍，你把它放下了。经济学家管这个叫机会成本。
- oc01-02 | The $2 you can see. The $1 you can't. The real price of the ice pop is the two together.
  > 2 美元看得见，1 美元看不见。冰棍真正的价钱，是两样加起来。
- oc01-03 | In those 30 minutes, you could also read a book, take a nap, or play soccer. But 30 minutes is only enough for one of them, so the opportunity cost is only the one you wanted most, not all of them added up.
  > 那三十分钟，你也可以看书、睡午觉、踢球。可三十分钟只够做一件事，所以机会成本只算你最想要的那一个，不是全部加起来。
- oc01-04 | Even if the ice pop were free, that dollar would still be gone. That's why economists say: there's no such thing as a free lunch.
  > 就算冰棍是免费的，那 1 美元也还是没有了。所以经济学家常说：天下没有免费的午餐。
- oc01-05 | And the same ice pop has a different price for different people: a kid who lives next door gets there in 2 minutes; for a kid who lives far away, the ice pop costs more.
  > 同一根冰棍，对不同的人，价钱也不一样：住在小卖部隔壁的孩子，走两分钟就到；住得远的孩子，这根冰棍就更贵。

## OC02 · Learning something is the same

- oc02-01 | Learning something is the same. Every Saturday afternoon, Mary has a one-hour piano lesson. The fee you can see; what you can't see is what that hour could have been.
  > 学一样东西也一样。Mary 每个星期六下午学一小时钢琴。学费看得见；看不见的，是这一小时本来可以做的事。
- oc02-02 | Everyone can hear the pieces she learns to play; the things she didn't do, nobody sees.
  > 她弹会的曲子，大家都听得见；没有做的事，谁也看不见。
- oc02-03 | In 1850, the French economist Frédéric Bastiat wrote an essay called “That Which Is Seen, and That Which Is Not Seen”. He said a bad economist sees only what can be seen; a good economist also thinks about what can't.
  > 1850 年，法国经济学家巴斯夏写过一篇文章，叫《看得见的与看不见的》。他说，不好的经济学家只看见看得见的；好的经济学家，还会去想那些看不见的。
- oc02-04 | A day has only 24 hours, like 24 time coins, and each coin can be spent only once. Sleep, school and meals use up most of them; the ones left for you to choose are often just three or four.
  > 一天只有 24 个小时，就像 24 枚时间币，每一枚只能花一次。睡觉、上学、吃饭花掉一大半，剩下能自己选的，往往只有三四枚。
- oc02-05 | Because there isn't enough time, every choice has a cost. Economists call this <strong>scarcity</strong>.
  > 时间不够用，选择才有代价。经济学管这个叫稀缺。
- oc02-06 | So when parents choose classes for their children, they can put the unseen half on the scale too. One more class brings things you can see: a fee and a new skill. What you can't see is what that afternoon would have been: maybe free play, maybe a family dinner.
  > 所以爸爸妈妈帮孩子选课的时候，也可以把看不见的那一半放上天平：多报一个班，看得见的是学费和本领；看不见的，是那个下午原本会做的事，也许是自由地玩，也许是一家人一起吃顿饭。
- oc02-07 | This doesn't mean you shouldn't learn. It means that when you choose, you see both sides.
  > 这不是说不该学，而是选的时候，两边都看见。

## OC03 · Before you know the answer

- oc03-01 | In learning, there's another price you can't see, and it hides even deeper.
  > 学习里，还有一种看不见的价钱，藏得更深。
- oc03-02 | When you can't solve a problem yet, you have to think and find a way. Finding the way teaches you a lot.
  > 一道题还不会的时候，你得自己动脑筋，想办法。想办法的过程，会教给你很多东西。
- oc03-03 | But once you've memorized the answer, you stop thinking about it. The ideas you could have learned become the things you gave up.
  > 可如果答案已经背下来了，你就不会再去想了。本来能学到的那些想法，就成了你放下的东西。
- oc03-04 | Let's look at a problem everyone knows: 7 times 8.
  > 我们拿一道大家最熟的题来看看：7 乘 8。

## OC04 · 7 × 8: how many paths?

- oc04-01 | Before second grade, Mary already knew her times tables by heart. Her teacher asked: what is 7 times 8?
  > Mary 还没上二年级，就把乘法口诀背得滚瓜烂熟。老师问：7 乘 8 等于多少？
- oc04-02 | Mary: Seven eights are fifty-six!
  > 七八五十六！
- oc04-03 | Then her teacher asked: how did you work it out?
  > 老师又问：你是怎么算出来的？
- oc04-04 | Mary: That's what the times table says!
  > 口诀里就是这么说的呀！
- oc04-05 | The answer is right. But if you hadn't learned the times table yet, how would you work out 7 times 8? There are actually several paths. Let's draw them all on the same dot picture: 8 rows, with 7 dots in each row.
  > 答案是对的。可如果还没背过口诀，7 乘 8，你会怎么算？其实有好几条路。我们都画在同一张点子图上：8 行，每行 7 个点。
- oc04-06 | Path one: add equal groups. Add it up row by row: 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49, 56. What it trains: what multiplying really means.
  > 第一条路：连加。一行一行加起来：7、14、21、28、35、42、49、56。它练的是：乘法到底是什么。
- oc04-07 | Path two: split and join. Cut it from top to bottom: on the left, 8 times 5 is 40; on the right, 8 times 2 is 16; together, 56. What it trains: breaking a hard one into easy ones. Later, that's exactly how you'll multiply two-digit numbers.
  > 第二条路：拆开再合。竖着切一刀，左边 8 乘 5 是 40，右边 8 乘 2 是 16，合起来是 56。它练的是：把难的拆成好算的。以后的两位数乘法，就是这么算的。
- oc04-08 | Path three: use your fingers, counting back from 10, and you get 56 too. Why that works gets a video of its own.
  > 第三条路，用手指：从 10 往回看，也能算出 56。为什么可以这样算，我们单独用一个视频来讲。
- oc04-15 | Path four: only doubling and halving. Over three thousand years ago, the ancient Egyptians multiplied by doubling.
  > 第四条路，只用翻倍和减半。三千多年前的古埃及人，算乘法就是靠翻倍。
- oc04-16 | Write 7 on the left and halve it each time, dropping any remainder: 7, 3, 1. Write 8 on the right and double it each time: 8, 16, 32. Wherever the left side is odd, keep the right side. All three rows are odd: 8 plus 16 plus 32 equals 56.
  > 左边写 7，每次减半，去掉余数：7、3、1。右边写 8，每次翻倍：8、16、32。左边是单数的行，右边就留下。这三行都是单数：8 加 16 加 32，等于 56。
- oc04-17 | Why? Turn the dot picture on its side: 7 rows of 8.
  > 为什么？把点子图横过来：7 行，每行 8 个。
- oc04-18 | 7 is odd, so first take away 1 row: that's 8. The 6 rows left pair up into 3 groups of 16.
  > 7 是单数，先拿走 1 行，是 8。剩下 6 行，两行并成一份，变成 3 份，每份 16。
- oc04-19 | 3 is odd, so take away 1 group: that's 16. The 2 groups left join into 1 group of 32, and we take that too.
  > 3 是单数，再拿走 1 份，是 16。剩下 2 份，并成 1 份，是 32，也拿走。
- oc04-20 | Halving on the left counts the groups; doubling on the right is how big each group is; whenever the count is odd, take one group away. 8 plus 16 plus 32: not a single dot is missing.
  > 左边减半，是份数；右边翻倍，是每份有多大；遇到单数，就拿走一份。8 加 16 加 32，一个点都不少。
- oc04-21 | What it trains: doubling alone can build any number. The binary numbers inside a computer work the same way.
  > 它练的是：只用翻倍，也能拼出任何一个数。计算机里的二进制，用的就是这个道理。
- oc04-22 | Four paths, four ways of thinking: what multiplying means, breaking it into easy parts, counting back from 10, and building any number by doubling.
  > 四条路，四种想法：乘法的意思，拆成好算的，从 10 往回看，用翻倍拼出任何数。
- oc04-23 | You only walk these paths while you don't know the answer yet. Mary memorized “seven eights are fifty-six” first, so she won't walk them anymore.
  > 这些路，都要在还不知道答案的时候，才会去走。Mary 先背下了七八五十六，就不会再去走了。
- oc04-24 | What she gave up is these four ways of thinking. That's <strong>opportunity cost</strong> too, only it's extra hard to see, because the answer is right.
  > 她放下的，就是这四种想法。这也是机会成本，只是它特别难看见，因为答案是对的。

## OC05 · Walk the paths first, then learn the table

- oc05-01 | This doesn't mean you shouldn't learn the times table. Knowing it well makes bigger problems faster later on.
  > 这不是说不要背口诀。口诀背得熟，以后算大题才快。
- oc05-02 | It's just that the order matters: first think for yourself and walk these paths, then learn the table by heart. Then “seven eights are fifty-six” won't just be a chant; you'll know where it comes from.
  > 只是顺序很重要：先自己动脑筋，走一走这些路，再把口诀背熟。那时候，「七八五十六」就不只是一句顺口溜，你知道它是从哪里来的。
- oc05-03 | Today we found two prices you can't see: when you choose, it's the thing you put down, like that one dollar; when you learn, it's the ideas you give up by going straight to the answer.
  > 今天我们找到了两种看不见的价钱：选择的时候，是放下的那件事，就像那 1 美元；学习的时候，是直接拿答案，放下的那些想法。
- oc05-04 | Seeing what can't be seen: that's an economist's eye, and a good learner's eye too.
  > 看见看不见的，这是经济学家的眼睛，也是会学习的人的眼睛。
- oc05-05 | Oh, and if you're thinking: I've already studied for three years, so if I switch now, wasn't it all for nothing? The time you've spent won't come back, so should it decide your next step? That's called a <strong>sunk cost</strong>, and we'll talk about it next time.
  > 对了，如果你想：我已经学了三年，现在换，前面不就白学了？已经花掉的时间拿不回来，它该不该决定下一步怎么走呢？这叫沉没成本，我们下一次再聊。

## OC06 · Easter egg: Martha and Mary

- oc06-01 | By the way, there was a Mary two thousand years ago, too. She and her sister faced a choice just like this.
  > 说起来，Mary 这个名字，两千年前就有一位。她和姐姐，也遇到过这样的选择。
- oc06-02 | Jesus came to their home. Her sister Martha was busy getting the meal ready, while Mary sat at Jesus' feet and listened to him. Martha couldn't keep up, so she asked Jesus to tell her sister to help.
  > 耶稣来到她们家。马大忙着准备饭菜；马利亚坐在耶稣脚前听道。马大忙不过来，就请耶稣叫妹妹来帮忙。
- oc06-03 | Jesus said: “Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is needed. Mary has chosen the good part, which will not be taken away from her.”
  > 耶稣说：「马大！马大！你为许多的事思虑烦扰，但是不可少的只有一件；马利亚已经选择那上好的福分，是不能夺去的。」
- oc06-04 | Welcoming guests is good; sitting down to listen is good too. The hardest choices are often between two good things. When the thing you put down is good too, the opportunity cost is highest.
  > 招待客人是好事，坐下来听也是好事。最难的选择，往往在两件好事之间。被放下的那件也很好的时候，机会成本最高。
