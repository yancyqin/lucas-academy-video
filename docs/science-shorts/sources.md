# Short-film source record

Both films use existing owner-made Muse clips. No new downloads, generated clips or still images are needed. The optogenetics originals were restored from `../lucas-academy-video-data/fun-informatics-04/muse/` and checked against that backup’s SHA256SUMS. All moving-source hashes and in/out windows are recorded in assets.json and the two timelines.

The perceptron figures come only from the selected original `scripts/perceptron-race/results/*.json`. No benchmark, training rerun or contender call is made. The held-out input, trained points and419 training paths come from the preserved full-film inputs. Its speed is shown at real elapsed seconds; source-footage playback speed is independent of the race clock. The caveat about specialized speed versus overall ability remains spoken and visible.

The optogenetics short follows the previously made Fun Informatics4 film, concentrating on a light-sensitive door, surviving retinal relay cells, special goggles, encoding/decoding and the2021 patient case. It does not describe or imitate what the patient saw. The notebook and goggles clips are illustrative reconstructions. Blue-light gating in the general diagram is separate from the amber-sensitive switch used in that patient. The narration and picture both retain one patient, partial recovery, glasses still needed, and normal vision not restored.

Primary verification:

- Sahel et al., *Partial recovery of visual function in a blind patient after optogenetic therapy*, Nature Medicine27,1223–1229(2021), DOI10.1038/s41591-021-01351-4.
